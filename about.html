About this battle room

This local app reuses the supplied Daily Red battle engine, damage calculator, seeded randomness and opponent AI. “Daily Red” is the original uploaded Red team; the expanded player pool has 214 distinct species and 568 naturally trained builds. The other 32 profiles are also available.

Choose a trainer and variant, a pool and a date, then select your lead. Click a move to attack or a party member to switch. Required replacements do not add a battle turn. Your exact teams, seed and choices are saved in this browser. Continue saved battle restores them by replaying those choices. Download replay keeps an independent copy.

Opponent-balanced parties pass type-variety, shared-weakness, offensive-coverage and power-budget checks before being tested against the selected opponent in the original engine. All-Water teams and stacks of high-power Pokémon are rejected. Unrestricted random bypasses these checks. A winning route is replay-verified before a party is marked verified. If the bounded check finds no win, the app says so. Unrestricted random still draws species first, then builds, with the same dated draw across opponents. Screening changes the final species odds; it does not change legal moves, stats or battle rules. Practice generates a fresh recorded seed. These are local challenges: they do not submit scores to daily-red.com or consume your official daily battle. Replay this challenge repeats the same seed.

Builds use Generation 3 level-up moves, with delayed evolution, native Move Reminders, and Sketch where appropriate. Natures and EV training suit their roles. Their acquisition records assume access to the relevant species, trades and events; the app does not simulate obtaining them on a cartridge. No claim of globally optimal teams is made. The original damage-focused AI can underuse support moves.

Battle play works without external services. Pokémon sprites load from the PokeAPI sprites repository when available; offline or missing artwork falls back to a Pokédex number. Art and Pokémon content belong to their respective owners. The imported third-party engine is kept separate from original app code; preserve its notices.
